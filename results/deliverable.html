Laguerre irreducibility for 50 < a ≤ 1000

The computations agree with Theorem 1.1 of the paper. The theorem: for 50 < a ≤ 1000 and n ≥ 2, Ln(a)(x) is irreducible over Q, except when n = 2 and a + 2 is a perfect square, where it has a linear factor.

This is a re-run of the finite computations of the paper's proof, not an independent proof. The lemmas that reduce the problem to these computations, and the results listed under “Imports”, are taken as given. It has not been reviewed by the paper's authors.

Imports: assumed, not verified here

  • Lemmas 2.1, 2.2, 2.3 and 2.10 (Laishram–Shorey, Nair–Shorey, Najman), and the deduction of Lemma 5.2 from them: no factor of degree k ≥ 6.
  • Lehmer's Tables IA and IB: no m > 1,611,308,700 has P(m(m−1)) ≤ 31. Our enumeration agrees up to 10¹⁰.
  • Szegő's bounds on the zeros of Laguerre polynomials (Lemma 6.4).
  • The whole range 0 ≤ a ≤ 50 (Laishram–Shorey). Its 8 exceptions were spot-checked.
  • The proofs of the filter lemmas 4.1–4.3, 5.1, 5.3, 5.4, 6.1–6.3 and of Proposition 1.3.

Differences from the paper

None affects the theorem: every case that survives the filters here is closed by a certificate. A difference may come from a step the paper does not spell out, or from a misreading on my side.

Stage D: pairs (n, k) with P(Delta(n-k+1,k)) < 11 differs, explainedPaper: 10 items · ours: 11 items
The paper says P(Delta(n-k+1,k)) >= 11 for all n >= 11 except (16, 3). But (n, k) = (50, 3) gives 48 * 49 * 50, whose largest prime is 7. The paper's count 186 for k = 3 matches ours only without n = 50, so this pair does not seem to be covered by the paper's counts. It is treated here in Stage F like the other small pairs (38 extra cases, all closed by factorisation).
Stage F: number of distinct (n, a) for the paper's small pairs differs, openPaper: 97 · ours: 228
UNEXPLAINED. The 228 pairs for k = 3 are already distinct, so the number of distinct (n, a) cannot be below 228. We factorise all of them, so nothing is lost.
Stage D, group at most 5: survivors with n = 81 differs, explainedPaper: [[81, 115], [81, 235], [81, 544], [81, 648]] · ours: 6 items
Lemma 5.4 as stated does not eliminate a = 144 and a = 360: 225 * 224 has no prime >= 11, and for 441 * 440 the prime 11 satisfies 11^2 | (a + 3). The paper does not say how it removes them. Both are closed below by a Newton polygon (p = 3, right-most slope 40/81 < 1/2).
Part A: the set S-tilde differs, explainedPaper: 19 items · ours: 20 items
We get one extra pair, (30, 699). It passes Lemmas 6.1, 6.2, 6.3 and 5.4 as we read them: 5^2 | (a+1), 3^3 | (a+3) with nu_3(n) = 1, and n + a = 3^6. No Newton polygon excludes it either (the right-most slope is >= 1 for p = 2, 3, 5). It is closed by factorisation: the polynomial is irreducible.
(n, a) = (896, 979): candidates left by Proposition 1.3 (paper: xi = 2770, not a root) differs, explainedPaper: [2770] · ours: []
Our candidate list also requires Rad(n(n+a)) | xi (Remark 2 of the paper). Here Rad(896 * 1875) = 210 does not divide 2770, so 2770 is already excluded and no candidate is left. The conclusion (no integer root) is the same.
(n, a) = (400, 624): number of candidate xi checked (reading uncertain) differs, openPaper: 128 · ours: 270
UNEXPLAINED. We count all multiples of Rad(n(n+a)) = 10 in (0, 2706]. Other readings we tried give 142 (xi sharing a factor with (n-1)(n+a-1)), 124, 112 and 94. All 270 candidates are tested and none is a root, so nothing is lost.

Other findings

Lemma 6.6, the count 366Reproduced exactly only when the p = 3 condition of Lemma 5.4(ii) is left out. With it, 296 pairs remain, and (768, 480) from the paper's list of 11 large pairs drops out.
Lemma 6.1(iii), statement against proofThe statement and the proof differ in two places. The paper's own list contains (576, 482), which the literal statement would remove, so the proof's version is the one consistent with that list.
Lemma 4.3, “r₂ ≠ p”The proof only excludes “r₂ = p and φ⁽²⁾ ≥ 1/k”. On the polygon alone r₂ = p does occur: (n, a, k, p) = (352, 845, 2, 11) has r₁ = 2, r₂ = 11, φ⁽²⁾ = 1/9. Nothing downstream needs it.
